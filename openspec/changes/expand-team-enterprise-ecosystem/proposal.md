## Why

After Developer Monitor demonstrates retention, broader adoption will depend on collaboration,
organizational controls, ecosystem coverage, and deployment choices. These capabilities should
follow demonstrated demand rather than being bundled into the first paid release.

## What Changes

- Add shared workspaces, workflow ownership, roles, audit trails, and centralized policy management.
- Add Slack or Teams notifications and OpenTelemetry export.
- Add Python and high-demand framework adapters while preserving report parity.
- Add enterprise identity, retention controls, private networking, customer-managed encryption,
  custom catalogues, support commitments, and a self-hosted deployment option.
- Gate each expansion on repeated customer evidence and publish compatibility/support policies.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `team-governance`: Shared workspaces, ownership, access control, auditing, and centralized policy.
- `ecosystem-integrations`: Notification, telemetry, language, and framework integrations that
  preserve deterministic analysis and contract compatibility.
- `enterprise-deployment`: Enterprise identity, data controls, private connectivity, custom pricing,
  support, and self-hosted operation.

### Modified Capabilities

None.

## Impact

Primarily the private control plane plus new public SDK/adaptor packages and integration contracts.
Some capabilities require paid third-party services and always-on infrastructure; each must remain
optional and isolated from the static Community path. Packages that perform local analysis remain
open, deterministic, and usable without Monitor.
